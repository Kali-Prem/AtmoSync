## 1. Plume Transport Dynamics: From Punjab to Delhi NCR

Plumes generated by biomass burning in Punjab and Haryana undergo a multi-stage physical evolution as they traverse the 250–350 km transport corridor toward Delhi NCR:
1. **Buoyant Plume Rise & Injection:** Sensible heat from intense farm fires lofts smoke into the atmospheric boundary layer ($400\text{ m} - 1,200\text{ m}$ AGL).
2. **Lagrangian Advection:** Wind vectors at $850\text{ hPa}$ and $10\text{ m}$ steer the air mass southeastward along the terrain gradient of the Indo-Gangetic Plain.
3. **Turbulent Diffusion & Broadening:** Cross-wind ($\sigma_y$) and vertical ($\sigma_z$) turbulent eddies disperse the smoke cone, reducing peak core concentration while expanding the geographic footprint.
4. **Dry Deposition & Secondary Aging:** Larger ash particles deposit gravimetrically; volatile organic compounds (VOCs) photo-oxidize into Secondary Organic Aerosols (SOA).
5. **Nocturnal Trapping & Fumigation:** When the plume arrives over the Delhi basin at night, the collapsing nocturnal boundary layer traps the advected smoke near the ground, causing severe morning particulate spikes.

## 3. Clear Distinction: Rigorous Science vs. Prototype Approximation

To uphold strict scientific integrity in Problem Statement 26082, we explicitly differentiate between what constitutes a full scientific simulation and what is implemented in the hackathon prototype:

### 3.1 The Scientifically Rigorous Approach (National Operational Standard)
- Solves 3D advection-diffusion-reaction partial differential equations:
  $$\frac{\partial C_i}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{u} C_i) + \nabla \cdot (\mathbf{K} \nabla C_i) + R_i(C_1, \dots, C_m) + E_i - S_i$$
- Couples continuous terrain-following coordinates with hourly meteorological grids.
- Simulates tens of thousands of stochastic Lagrangian particles with random-walk turbulence (e.g., HYSPLIT or FLEXPART).
- Requires high-performance computing clusters and multi-gigabyte wind fields.

### 3.2 The SIH Prototype Approach (Implementation-Ready Hybrid)
- **What it IS:** A physically sound, computationally agile **Forward Lagrangian Segmented Gaussian Puff Engine**. It calculates actual coordinates of advected smoke parcels, applies atmospheric stability-dependent Briggs dispersion coefficients, computes arrival Estimated Time of Arrival (ETA), and quantifies downwind ground-level mass burden ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$) entering Delhi NCR.
- **What it is NOT:** It is not an uncoupled drawing of static wind arrows on a map, nor is it a full 3D multi-chemical reactive Eulerian transport model.
- **Labeling Rule:** The system explicitly labels its live plume visualization on the dashboard as:  
  `"Lagrangian Forward Puff Dispersion Model (Near-Real-Time Operational Estimate)"`.

## 4. Mathematical Formulation of the Prototype Engine

### Step 1: Discrete Fire Cluster Source Initialization
Active fires detected by NASA FIRMS VIIRS are clustered via DBSCAN into $K$ major source nodes. Each cluster $k$ at initial location $(x_{0,k}, y_{0,k})$ has total Fire Radiative Power $FRP_k$ and emission rate $E_k = C_e \cdot FRP_k$ ($\text{kg/s}$).

### Step 2: Time-Stepped Forward Advection
Every hour $t \in [1, 72]$, discrete puffs are released from cluster centers and transported forward by the forecasted $10\text{m} - 850\text{hPa}$ wind vector field:

$$x_{k}(t+\Delta t) = x_k(t) + U(x_k, y_k, t) \cdot \Delta t$$
$$y_{k}(t+\Delta t) = y_k(t) + V(x_k, y_k, t) \cdot \Delta t$$

where $U, V$ are the interpolated horizontal wind components from the numerical weather forecast grid.

### Step 3: Spatial Diffusion (Gaussian Puff Expansion)
As each puff travels a downwind distance $d(t) = \sum \sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2}$, its spatial standard deviations ($\sigma_y, \sigma_z$) expand according to **Briggs Urban/Rural Dispersion Formulations** for atmospheric stability class (Pasquill-Gifford Class D/E for typical autumn conditions):

$$\sigma_y(t) = c_1 \cdot d(t) \cdot (1 + c_2 \cdot d(t))^{-1/2}$$
$$\sigma_z(t) = c_3 \cdot d(t) \cdot (1 + c_4 \cdot d(t))^{c_5}$$

### Step 4: Ground-Level Concentration Footprint
The ground-level particulate concentration contribution $C_{\text{puff}}$ at receptor coordinates $(x, y)$ from a puff with mass $M = E_k \cdot \Delta t$ at height $H_{\text{inj}}$ is:

$$C_{\text{puff}}(x, y, 0) = \frac{2 M}{(2\pi)^{3/2} \sigma_y^2 \sigma_z} \exp\left( -\frac{(x - x_k)^2 + (y - y_k)^2}{2\sigma_y^2} \right) \exp\left( -\frac{H_{\text{inj}}^2}{2\sigma_z^2} \right) \cdot \exp(-\lambda \cdot t)$$

where $\exp(-\lambda \cdot t)$ accounts for dry and wet deposition loss (half-life $\tau_{1/2} \approx 48\text{ hours}$).

### Step 5: Boundary Intersection & Delhi Mass Loading
The engine tests geometric intersection between puff expansion polygons and the Delhi NCR boundary polygon using PostGIS / Shapely:
- When intersection occurs, the system records:
  1. **Plume ETA:** Elapsed time from release to Delhi border arrival (hours).
  2. **Projected Contribution:** $\Delta PM_{2.5}^{\text{plume}}(t)$ ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$) added to Delhi's regional air mass.
  3. **Plume Risk Level:** Low ($<25\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$), Moderate ($25-60\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$), High ($60-120\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$), Extreme ($>120\,\mu\text{g/m}^3$).

This calculated $\Delta PM_{2.5}^{\text{plume}}$ serves as an explicit input feature into the machine learning downscaling engine, bridging physical transport directly into the forecast.
